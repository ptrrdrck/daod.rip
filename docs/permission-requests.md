# Permission requests — drafts

**Status: none of these have been sent.** They are here for review.

Three publishers, chosen because they hold the translations readers most want
to see and are the most likely to answer a small, specific request: Copper
Canyon (Red Pine), Shambhala (Le Guin) and HarperCollins (Mitchell).

## Before sending

- [ ] Find the current permissions contact for each publisher. Every one of
      them publishes a permissions page; use the address there rather than a
      general enquiries inbox, which is where these requests go to die.
- [ ] Fill in the Instagram handle once it exists. Sending this before there
      is an account to look at makes the request harder to say yes to.
- [ ] Decide whether to say the account is monetised. If affiliate links are
      live by then, say so — it is a point in your favour, and finding out
      later is worse than being told now.
- [ ] Send them one at a time, a week apart. If the first reply corrects an
      assumption, the other two are better for it.

## What is actually being asked

Short excerpts — a few lines, not whole chapters — in social posts that name
the translator, the year and the publisher, and link to buy the book. Not a
licence to republish. Keeping the ask small is what makes it answerable
without a contract.

Each draft also offers to remove the translation from the site. That is not a
throwaway line: it is what makes the rest of the email credible, and it should
only be sent by someone willing to follow through.

## 1. Copper Canyon Press — Red Pine (Bill Porter)

> **Subject:** Permission request — brief Taoteching excerpts with attribution
>
> Hello,
>
> I run daod.rip, a small non-commercial site that shows the same chapter of
> the Daodejing in several English translations at once, so a reader can see

> side-by-side form. The account will run on public-domain translations by
> default. I am writing because the comparison is far more interesting when a
> modern translation is in it, and Red Pine's is the one readers ask about.
>
> What I would like permission for is narrow: excerpts of a few lines at a
> time — not whole chapters — in posts that name Red Pine, Copper Canyon
> Press and the year, and link to buy the book. If it helps, I am glad to
> agree a maximum number of lines per post, to send the posts for approval
> before they run, or to stop at any point on request.
>
> If the answer is no, that is a complete answer and I will keep the account
> to public-domain translations. And if you would rather the translation not
> appear on daod.rip at all, tell me and I will take it down — I would rather
> know than assume.
>
> Thank you for your time, and for keeping this translation in print.
>
> Peter Rodrick
> pete@lftlc.xyz · daod.rip

## If one is granted

Record the grant and its terms in `RIGHTS.md`, then relax that translation's
status in `js/catalog.js`. A permission with conditions attached — a line
limit, an approval step — is a condition on the renderer too, not just on the
person writing captions, so it needs to end up somewhere the code can see it.
